Mira vs building your own Raspberry Pi smart mirror

Updated 12 July 2026 · 7 min read

Mira actually runs on a Raspberry Pi — so we're the last people to talk you out of a DIY build. But there's a big gap between a clock-on-glass weekend project and a finished AI companion. Here's the honest trade-off.

The DIY route (MagicMirror² on a Pi)

The open-source MagicMirror² platform is fantastic. On a Raspberry Pi with a spare monitor and two-way acrylic, you can display time, weather, calendar and news for roughly ₹5,000–12,000 in parts. The community is huge and the customisation is unlimited.

Where it gets hard is everything past the display:

  • Voice AI — wiring a wake word, speech-to-text, an LLM and text-to-speech together, reliably, is a real project.
  • Face recognition — camera, models, per-user profiles, edge cases in low light.
  • Fitness, music control, messaging — each is its own integration to build and maintain.
  • Security camera — motion detection, alerts, storage, privacy handling.
  • Reliability & updates — when an API changes or the Pi hangs, you're the support team.

None of it is impossible — it's just time, often many weekends, and ongoing upkeep.

The Mira route

Mira is that same Raspberry Pi foundation, but with the hard 90% already built, tested and supported: Claude AI for real conversation, wake-word voice, Google Calendar, Spotify, WhatsApp, an 8-workout fitness mode, face recognition for up to 6 people, and an on-device security camera — plus a companion app and a 3-minute QR setup. You plug it in and it works.

So which should you do?

Build DIY if the project is the point — you like electronics and code, and a clock/weather/calendar mirror (maybe with some tinkering toward voice) is what you want.

Get Mira if you want the finished companion — real AI, fitness, security, a polished app — without becoming your own R&D and support department.

A fair way to think about it: DIY buys you a display cheaply; Mira buys you an assistant that happens to be a mirror. The parts cost is similar; the finished software and support are the difference.
