Best AI smart mirrors in India (2026)

Updated 12 July 2026 · 7 min read

"Smart mirror" in India covers three very different things: consumer AI mirrors, vanity/bathroom LED mirrors with voice, and DIY builds. This guide compares the options that make sense for a home in 2026 — honestly, including where we (Mira) do and don't fit.

Full disclosure: this guide is published by Mira. We've tried to be fair — Mira is pre-launch (shipping 2026), and the alternatives below are real products you can buy today. We tell you exactly who each one is best for, including when it isn't us.

1. Mira — the AI companion mirror

Best for: someone who wants the mirror to genuinely run their morning — not just show a clock.

Mira is a consumer smart mirror powered by Claude AI, so it holds a real conversation rather than matching fixed commands. Say "Hey Mira" and it handles your Google Calendar, plays Spotify, reads WhatsApp messages, runs guided workouts (8 built-in, with timers and calorie tracking), and recognises your face to load your personal dashboard. A built-in camera adds motion alerts and live viewing, with footage kept on-device. Built in Pune; ships 2026 in a limited first batch, expected in the ₹15,000–40,000 range.

Trade-off: it's pre-launch — you join a waitlist rather than buy today.

2. Ricovi Smart Mirror (with "Genie")

Best for: a beautiful bathroom or vanity mirror with helpful voice extras, available now.

Sold on Amazon.in, the Ricovi S-Series is a gold-framed mirror with an AI voice assistant called Genie. It does real-time weather, news, music via dual 5W speakers, a voice-controlled defogger, adjustable lighting modes, and a proximity sensor that wakes it as you approach. It's genuinely nice hardware.

Trade-off: Genie is a command-based assistant, not a conversational AI — and it's focused on grooming/vanity, so there's no calendar dashboard, fitness coaching, or security camera. It's a great mirror with voice, not a daily companion.

3. MirroCool

Best for: notifications-and-assistant basics with face recognition.

MirroCool is a consumer smart mirror that pairs with your phone and includes a built-in HD camera that recognises registered profiles. It leans toward personal-assistant and notification features.

Trade-off: it's a global product with limited India presence and support, and its assistant depth is lighter than a modern LLM-based system.

4. DIY Raspberry Pi / ESP32 magic mirror

Best for: hobbyists who enjoy the build as much as the result.

With MagicMirror² on a Raspberry Pi (or an ESP32 for a basic clock/weather display), you can build a mirror for as little as ₹2,000–5,000 in parts. It's the cheapest route and endlessly customisable.

Trade-off: you're the manufacturer and the support team. Voice AI, face recognition, security and a polished companion app are weeks of work each. Great hobby; not plug-and-play. We wrote a full Mira vs DIY comparison if you're weighing this.

  • Want a gorgeous bathroom mirror with voice, today? Ricovi.
  • Love building things and have time? DIY.
